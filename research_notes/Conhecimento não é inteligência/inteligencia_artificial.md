## 1. Chollet's "On the Measure of Intelligence" (2019): intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency, and why skill/knowledge can be "bought"

### Takeaway
Chollet (2019) gives the most direct AI-side statement of "conhecimento ≠ inteligência". Skill on a task is "heavily modulated by prior knowledge and experience", so unlimited priors or data let you "buy" arbitrary skill. In his view intelligence is the *efficiency* with which a system turns limited priors and experience into new skill on tasks it was not prepared for.

### Cited Findings
- **Full citation:** François Chollet (Google), "On the Measure of Intelligence", arXiv:1911.01547, submitted November 2019 (v2 later). Not peer-reviewed in a conference or journal. It introduced the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus (ARC). — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01547); [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.01547v2); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-the-Measure-of-Intelligence-Chollet/261c25adc19c2d107403913e464ad5520a56a261)
- **Core "buying skill" quote (abstract; the "unlimited priors" and "buy" wording was confirmed in search extracts of several copies of the abstract; note it is "skills", plural):** "We argue that solely measuring skill at any given task falls short of measuring intelligence, because skill is heavily modulated by prior knowledge and experience: unlimited priors or unlimited training data allow experimenters to 'buy' arbitrary levels of skills for a system, in a way that masks the system's own generalization power." — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.01547v2)
- **Abstract, definition sentence:** "...We then articulate a new formal definition of intelligence based on Algorithmic Information Theory, describing intelligence as skill-acquisition efficiency and highlighting the concepts of scope, generalization difficulty, priors, and experience." — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01547) [wording from the canonical abstract; the paraphrase was confirmed by search, the exact sentence is unverified this session]
- **Formal-definition sentence (Section II.2, widely quoted):** "The intelligence of a system is a measure of its skill-acquisition efficiency over a scope of tasks, with respect to priors, experience, and generalization difficulty." — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01547); the paraphrase was confirmed in a secondary summary ([Wellacott summary](https://liamwellacott.github.io/artificial%20intelligence/on-the-measure-of-intelligence/)). The exact wording is from the canonical text [unverified this session].
- **Abstract, on contemporary AI practice:** the AI community "still gravitates towards benchmarking intelligence by comparing the skill exhibited by AIs and humans at specific tasks such as board games and video games." — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01547) [canonical abstract; unverified this session]
- **Abstract, ARC's purpose:** ARC is "built upon an explicit set of priors designed to be as close as possible to innate human priors... ARC can be used to measure a human-like form of general fluid intelligence and... enables fair general intelligence comparisons between AI systems and humans." — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.01547) [canonical abstract; unverified this session]
- **Chollet restating the distinction in interviews:** "In the words of Jean Piaget, intelligence is what you use when you don't know what to do." He also says "Memorization is useful, but intelligence is something else." (Freethink interview, republished by Big Think, 2024). — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/the-future/arc-prize-agi/)
- On the Dwarkesh Podcast (June 2024, with Mike Knoop, launching the $1M ARC Prize), the episode framing is that people are "confusing skill and intelligence". Chollet argues that a model trained on large numbers of similar puzzles is still using memorization, and he calls GSM8K a memorization benchmark. — [Dwarkesh Podcast](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/francois-chollet)

### Inferences
- Chollet's framework maps closely onto "conhecimento ≠ inteligência". *Knowledge* (priors plus accumulated experience) and *skill* are what a system has. *Intelligence* is the conversion rate from limited information to new competence. A system with huge knowledge and high skill can therefore score low on intelligence if that skill came from data volume rather than efficient generalization.
- The framework also implies the reverse: a system with little knowledge (a child) can be highly intelligent. That makes it a natural bridge to developmental psychology, and to the Piaget quote Chollet himself uses.

## 2. ARC / ARC-AGI benchmark history (2019 to Oct 2026): what it shows about vast knowledge vs. novel reasoning, and whether the gap narrowed

### Takeaway
Each ARC version followed the same arc. Pure LLMs with vast knowledge scored near zero at first. Gains then came from test-time adaptation, search and refinement loops, at very high initial cost, and scores then rose fast. ARC-AGI-1: GPT-4o about 5% (2024), then o3 75.7–87.5% (Dec 2024). ARC-AGI-2: pure LLMs 0% at launch (Mar 2025), then the 60% human-panel average was surpassed by early 2026. ARC-AGI-3: frontier models under 1% at launch (Mar 2026). The ARC Prize Foundation's own reading of 2025 is that frontier reasoning remains "constrained to knowledge coverage". So the gap narrows on each fixed benchmark, but it reopens when the novelty format changes.

### Inferences
- **Pattern for the thesis:** across three benchmark generations, systems with internet-scale knowledge started near 0% on tasks built to require novel abstraction from a few examples. Humans with no special knowledge did well (about 60% average and 100% collective coverage on ARC-AGI-2, and 100% of ARC-AGI-3 environments). This is the strongest empirical illustration in AI that encyclopedic knowledge does not by itself give fluid, novel-situation intelligence.
- **The gap has narrowed, in a specific way.** Gains came from *test-time adaptation*: TTT, program search, chain-of-thought RL and refinement loops, all mechanisms that learn per task at inference. More stored knowledge was not the driver. That fits Chollet's framing (intelligence = adaptation efficiency), even as it erodes the claim that "LLM-based systems can't do ARC".
- **The efficiency gap persists on cost:** o3 used up to about $4,560/task in 2024 against about $2–17/task for humans. The 2025 prize winners still needed hundreds of thousands of synthetic examples. Under Chollet's definition, high skill bought with enormous experience or compute counts for *less* intelligence, so cost and data efficiency are central to the argument and not a footnote.
- **The ARC team's own "knowledge coverage" conclusion is a direct, citable AI-side statement for the thesis.** It says even reasoning-model performance depends on the training distribution covering the relevant domain.
- **Caveat against overclaiming:** the rapid climb on ARC-AGI-2 (0% to over 80% in roughly a year, if the Deep Think figure holds) and early harness results on ARC-AGI-3 suggest the gap is a moving target. A fair report should say the evidence supports "knowledge is not *sufficient* for intelligence". It does not show that "knowledge-rich systems can't acquire intelligence".

## 3. Evidence that LLMs rely on memorization/retrieval rather than reasoning

### Takeaway
A substantial 2023–2025 literature shows LLM performance degrading when surface features move away from training-data frequency: reversed relations, rare cipher shifts, counterfactual rules, changed numbers and irrelevant clauses. This is direct evidence that much apparent "reasoning" is anchored to familiar patterns, i.e. knowledge-like retrieval. However, several effects shrink markedly in 2025–2026 reasoning models, and some headline results (Apple's "Illusion of Thinking", GSM-NoOp) have credible methodological rebuttals.

### Cited Findings
- **Reversal curse.** Berglund, Tong, Kaufmann, Balesni, Stickland, Korbak, Evans, "The Reversal Curse: LLMs trained on 'A is B' fail to learn 'B is A'", ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.12288. Models trained on "A is B" do not automatically generalize to "B is A". On real celebrity–parent pairs, GPT-4 answered questions like "Who is Tom Cruise's mother?" correctly **79%** of the time versus **33%** for the reverse ("Who is Mary Lee Pfeiffer's son?"). The authors caution that GPT-4's fine-tuning to avoid revealing personal information may deflate the reverse figure. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12288); [OpenReview (ICLR 2024)](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=TGTZwVabpU)
- **Embers of autoregression.** McCoy, Yao, Friedman, Hardy, Griffiths, "Embers of Autoregression: Understanding Large Language Models Through the Problem They are Trained to Solve", arXiv:2309.13638 (2023); published in PNAS 2024 [venue from memory, unverified this session]. Findings:
  - GPT-4's shift-cipher decoding scores ≥50% on the three most common shifts (1, 3, 13) "yet scores below 3% for all other shifts".
  - Accuracy is 51% when the *output* is a high-probability word sequence versus 13% when it is low-probability.
  - Rot-13 (common) beats rot-2 (rare) for GPT-3.5 and GPT-4.
  — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.13638); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2309.13638)
  - Follow-up (McCoy et al. 2024, arXiv:2410.01792): OpenAI o1 still shows these probability effects but "performed substantially better" than the earlier LLMs, "with particularly strong results on rare variants of common tasks". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.01792)
- **Counterfactual tasks.** Wu, Qiu, Ross, Akyürek, Chen, Wang, Kim, Andreas, Kim, "Reasoning or Reciting? Exploring the Capabilities and Limitations of Language Models Through Counterfactual Tasks", arXiv:2307.02477 (2023); NAACL 2024 [venue from memory]. Across 11 tasks with counterfactual variants (e.g., arithmetic in base 9 instead of base 10, altered conventions), "performance substantially and consistently degrades compared to the default conditions". LMs "may possess abstract task-solving skills to a degree" but "often also rely on narrow, non-transferable procedures". — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02477); discussed by Melanie Mitchell on her Substack: [Mitchell, "Evaluating Large Language Models Using 'Counterfactual Tasks'"](https://aiguide.substack.com/p/evaluating-large-language-models)
- **GSM-Symbolic.** Mirzadeh, Alizadeh, Shahrokhi, Tuzel, Bengio, Farajtabar (Apple), "GSM-Symbolic: Understanding the Limitations of Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models", ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.05229 (Oct 2024). Findings:
  - All models' performance drops on templated variants of GSM8K.
  - Models are fairly robust to changed names but "very sensitive to changes in numerical values".
  - Adding a single irrelevant but plausible clause (GSM-NoOp) causes drops of **up to 65%** across state-of-the-art models.
  - Few-shot examples do not fix this.
  - Secondary sources report GPT-4o falling from 94.9% to 63.0% on NoOp (not verified against the paper).
  — [arXiv PDF (ICLR 2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.05229); [Medium summary](https://ritvik19.medium.com/papers-explained-260-gsm-symbolic-759d379052c7)
  - **Counter-replication (2026):** a LessWrong re-test on 2026 frontier models found that the unfiltered NoOp drop matched the original paper. After auditing the distractor clauses, though, drops were "indistinguishable from zero". Apple never released the original GSM-NoOp dataset. — [LessWrong, "Revisiting GSM-Symbolic: Do 2026 Frontier Models Still Fail..."](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Ze4C99Dasj74YKCFh/revisiting-gsm-symbolic-do-2026-frontier-models-still-fail); [Sturgeon](https://www.benjaminsturgeon.com/inkhaven-day-8/)
- **Benchmark contamination: GSM1k.** Zhang et al. (Scale AI), "A Careful Examination of Large Language Model Performance on Grade School Arithmetic", NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks, arXiv:2405.00332. The authors built a fresh, human-written 1,000-problem set matched to GSM8K. Findings:
  - Some families (notably Phi and Mistral) dropped up to 8% (arXiv abstract; some versions and coverage say 13%).
  - Frontier models (GPT, Claude, Gemini) showed little or no drop.
  - A model's likelihood of generating GSM8K text correlated with its gap (Spearman r² ≈ 0.36), i.e. "partial memorization".
  — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00332); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/53384f2090c6a5cac952c598fd67992f-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks_Track.pdf)
- **"The Illusion of Thinking" (Apple, June 2025).** Shojaee, Mirzadeh, Alizadeh, Horton, Bengio, Farajtabar, "The Illusion of Thinking: Understanding the Strengths and Limitations of Reasoning Models via the Lens of Problem Complexity", arXiv:2506.06941. Using controllable puzzles (Tower of Hanoi, River Crossing, Blocks World, Checker Jumping), it reports that frontier large reasoning models face "complete accuracy collapse beyond certain complexities". Their reasoning effort rises with complexity and then declines despite token budget remaining, and supplying the solution algorithm did not help. — [arXiv:2506.06941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06941) [summary from my memory of the abstract; secondary coverage confirming the collapse claim: [Arize](https://arize.com/blog/the-illusion-of-thinking-what-the-apple-ai-paper-says-about-llm-reasoning/)]
- **Rebuttal: "The Illusion of the Illusion of Thinking: A Comment on Shojaee et al. (2025)",** arXiv:2506.09250 (June 2025). Bylined "C. Opus and A. Lawsen": Claude Opus as nominal co-author, with Alex Lawsen (Open Philanthropy) as main human author. The byline was not confirmed from the primary source. Its arguments:
  - The Tower of Hanoi failures coincide with models hitting output-token limits, and models sometimes say they are truncating ("to avoid making this too long, I'll stop here").
  - Some River Crossing instances (N≥6 with boat capacity 3) are mathematically unsolvable yet were scored as failures.
  - When asked for a program (e.g., a Lua function printing the 15-disk solution) instead of an exhaustive move list, models achieved high accuracy in under 5,000 tokens.
  — [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09250v2); [arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.09250v1); [HN discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44287172)
- **Abstraction vs. shortcut on ARC-like tasks (Mitchell group, Oct 2025).** Beger, Yi, Fu, Denton, Moskvichev, Tsai, Rajamanickam, Mitchell, "Do AI Models Perform Human-like Abstract Reasoning Across Modalities?", arXiv:2510.02125. The paper is motivated by o3-preview's human-level ARC-AGI-1 accuracy and tests models on ConceptARC. The best models' stated rules "are frequently based on surface-level 'shortcuts'" and capture the intended abstractions "considerably less often than humans". Accuracy alone "can substantially overestimate AI capabilities in textual modalities and underestimate them in visual modalities." — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02125); [HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02125v3)
- **Chollet's mechanistic claim:** "LLMs = 100% memorization. There is no other mechanism at work. A LLM is a curve fitted to a dataset (that is to say, a memory), with a sampling mechanism on top..." (X, Feb 2024). Also: "This is exactly what 'memorization' means... They memorize procedures (in the form of vector functions) that match their training data, and reapply those procedures at test time. And these procedures tend to generalize poorly..." (X, Nov 2024). — [X, Feb 2024](https://x.com/fchollet/status/1755270681359716611); [X, Nov 2024](https://x.com/fchollet/status/1860684083086438456)

### Inferences
- Taken together (reversal curse, embers, counterfactual tasks, GSM-Symbolic, ConceptARC shortcuts), the evidence shows a *knowledge-indexed* competence. Performance tracks how frequently a task form, output string or relation direction appeared in training, which is what a knowledge store does and not what a general reasoner should do. This is the clearest mechanistic AI evidence for "conhecimento ≠ inteligência".
- The **trend matters for the report:** reasoning-trained models (o1 onward) shrink several of these effects. Embers in o1 were "substantially better", and 2026 audited GSM-NoOp drops were near zero. A fair synthesis is that pre-2024 LLMs were largely knowledge-plus-pattern systems, and that RL-trained reasoning plus test-time compute adds something closer to procedural generalization, though it remains imperfect and costly.
- The "Illusion of Thinking" debate shows how hard it is to separate *capability* from *evaluation artifacts*. It should be presented as contested, not as proof.

### Gaps
- Exact per-task numbers from Wu et al. (base-9 arithmetic, etc.) not retrieved.
- Could not verify the venue for McCoy et al. (PNAS 2024) or Wu et al. (NAACL 2024) this session.
- Did not retrieve LiveCodeBench or other date-cutoff contamination studies, nor Apple's own response to the rebuttal.

## 4. Counter-evidence and counterarguments: reasoning models, IMO 2025, the Bitter Lesson, scaling, compression, Legg & Hutter

### Takeaway
The strongest counter-evidence is that RL-trained reasoning models reached gold-medal level at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) on unseen problems, in natural language, and have since surpassed human averages on ARC-AGI-2. Theoretical counterarguments (the Bitter Lesson, scaling laws, prediction = compression = understanding, and Legg & Hutter's goal-achievement definition) suggest that at scale the line between "knowledge" and "intelligence" may blur. Notably, the Bitter Lesson *also* supports the thesis in one respect: hand-encoded knowledge consistently lost to general learning and search methods.

### Cited Findings
- **IMO 2025 (July 2025):**
  - Google DeepMind's advanced Gemini Deep Think solved 5 of 6 problems for **35/42 points**, gold-medal standard. It was graded and **officially certified** by IMO coordinators, working end-to-end in natural language within the 4.5-hour limit. This was the first officially certified AI gold; in 2024, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry reached silver using formal (Lean) proofs.
  - OpenAI's experimental reasoning model also scored 35/42, but that result was self-reported and not IMO-certified. OpenAI announced about two days before DeepMind.
  - About 8% of human contestants earn gold; one source places the models in a tie for 27th of 630.
  — [Google DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/advanced-version-of-gemini-with-deep-think-officially-achieves-gold-medal-standard-at-the-international-mathematical-olympiad/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/google-deepminds-gemini-wins-mathematical-olympiad-gold-using-only-natural-language/); [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/ai-reasoning-math-olympiad-imo)
- **DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025):** showed that reasoning behaviours (long chain-of-thought, self-verification) can be induced largely by reinforcement learning on verifiable rewards. Later published in *Nature* (Sept 2025). — [arXiv:2501.12948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948) [details and Nature publication from memory; unverified this session]
- **ARC-AGI-2 human average surpassed (late 2025 / early 2026):** see Section 2. GPT-5.2 (X-High) got 52.9% at $1.90/task and Gemini 3 Deep Think a reported 84.6%. — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DX3EmhmwZjTYp9PBf/ai-performance-has-surpassed-a-human-baseline-on-arc-agi-2); [Living survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13372v1)
- **Chollet's own concession on o3 (Dec 2024):** o3 "represents a significant breakthrough in getting AI to adapt to novel tasks." — [Chollet on X](https://x.com/fchollet/status/1870169764762710376)
- **The Bitter Lesson.** Rich Sutton, "The Bitter Lesson", essay, March 13, 2019, incompleteideas.net.
  - Core claim: "general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin" (confirmed in search extract).
  - His explanation is Moore's law, "or rather its generalization of continued exponentially falling cost per unit of computation". Chess is the first example, where search plus special hardware beat knowledge-based methods.
  - Further canonical lines, verbatim [unverified this session]: AI researchers "have often tried to build knowledge into their agents"; this "always helps in the short term... but in the long run it plateaus and even inhibits further progress"; and "We want AI agents that can discover like we can, not which contain what we have discovered."
  — [Original essay](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html); [reproduction (Hugging Face)](https://huggingface.co/spaces/yourbench/demo/blob/main/frontend/public/the-bitter-lesson.html); [reading notes](https://publish.obsidian.md/pkc/Literature/Reading+notes/%40BitterLesson)
- **Scaling laws:** Kaplan et al. 2020, "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models" (arXiv:2001.08361), and Hoffmann et al. 2022, "Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models" (Chinchilla; arXiv:2203.15556). Loss falls as a smooth power law in parameters, data and compute. — [Kaplan arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361); [Hoffmann arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556) [not re-verified this session]
- **Compression ⇒ generalization:**
  - Sutskever (Dwarkesh Podcast, 2023): "Predicting the next token well means that you understand the underlying reality that led to the creation of that token." He argues that compressing the statistics of text requires understanding what in the world produces them. The quote's existence was confirmed via transcript copies on LessWrong/GreaterWrong; the full wording beyond the first sentence is unverified. — [GreaterWrong transcript comment](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/FZkAG8Hezub7pWRM9/on-dwarkesh-s-3rd-podcast-with-tyler-cowen/comment/kboh4TyxGYFtRjjS3); [Analytics Vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2023/04/openai-co-founder-chief-data-scientist-on-the-potential-of-agi/)
  - Related empirical work: Delétang et al., "Language Modeling Is Compression" (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2309.10668), and Huang et al., "Compression Represents Intelligence Linearly" (COLM 2024, arXiv:2404.09937), which reports a near-linear correlation between LLMs' compression efficiency and benchmark scores. — [Delétang arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668); [Huang arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09937) [findings from memory, unverified this session]
- **Legg & Hutter (2007).** Shane Legg and Marcus Hutter, "Universal Intelligence: A Definition of Machine Intelligence", *Minds and Machines* 17(4): 391–444, 2007; arXiv:0712.3329.
  - The authors distil "well known informal definitions of human intelligence given by experts" into the informal definition "Intelligence measures an agent's ability to achieve goals in a wide range of environments".
  - They formalize it mathematically: performance across all computable environments, weighted by simplicity, linked to AIXI. The formula "formally captures the concept of machine intelligence in the broadest reasonable sense".
  - The informal-definition wording is confirmed via a secondary summary and is in the paper body, not the abstract. A companion paper collected about 70 definitions of intelligence.
  — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.3329); [ANU repository](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/items/11f1a866-e648-413b-a6e4-fc2bdb0529b4); [RERO PDF](https://doc.rero.ch/record/315409/files/11023_2007_Article_9079.pdf); [summary](https://crystal.uta.edu/~park/post/universal-intelligence)
- **Counter-counterpoint from scaling proponents:** reports say Sutskever and LeCun both came to agree (2025) that scaling is "necessary but not sufficient" and that current LLM scaling is hitting limits. — [abZ Global](https://www.abzglobal.net/web-development-blog/ilya-sutskever-yann-lecun-and-the-end-of-just-add-gpus) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Legg & Hutter vs. Chollet is the key definitional fault line.** L&H measure *achievement across environments*, a skill-like outcome, so a system that knows a lot and performs well everywhere counts as intelligent. Chollet measures *efficiency of acquisition relative to priors and experience*, so knowledge-bought performance is discounted. Whether "conhecimento ≠ inteligência" holds partly depends on which definition one adopts. This should be stated explicitly in the report.
- The **Bitter Lesson cuts both ways**. It supports the thesis because *encoding* human knowledge (expert systems, hand-crafted chess heuristics) did not scale to intelligence, while learning and search did. It challenges Chollet-style skepticism because scaling learning on huge data keeps producing capabilities that earlier looked like they required "real" reasoning. Sutton's own line, "discover like we can, not... contain what we have discovered", is itself a knowledge-vs-intelligence distinction.
- IMO gold is strong evidence of *some* novel reasoning: the problems were unseen. But the models were trained on very large math corpora and used heavy test-time compute, so under Chollet's definition the efficiency question stays open.

### Gaps
- I did not verify specific 2026 olympiad or research-math results (e.g., IMO 2026 outcomes in July 2026, FrontierMath scores). The report should not claim anything about IMO 2026 without a source.
- Exact compression-paper figures (e.g., Delétang's ImageNet/LibriSpeech ratios) not verified.

## 5. Classic AI lessons: expert systems/Cyc and Moravec's paradox

### Takeaway
Classic AI is the historical "natural experiment" for the thesis. Cyc (begun 1984) tried to hand-encode common-sense knowledge to overcome brittleness, and expert systems hit the knowledge-acquisition bottleneck. Neither produced general intelligence. Moravec's paradox (1988) adds that what looks like "intelligence" (chess, IQ-test puzzles) is easy for machines, while what humans find effortless (perception, mobility) is hard.

### Cited Findings
- **Cyc:** Lenat, Prakash and Shepherd (1986), *AI Magazine*, "CYC: Using Common Sense Knowledge to Overcome Brittleness and Knowledge Acquisition Bottlenecks". The paper frames expert systems' core problem as brittleness "when confronted by problems that were not foreseen by its builders", together with the manpower needed to build them. It names "knowledge acquisition bottlenecks" as the central obstacle and proposes a huge common-sense knowledge base as the remedy. — [ResearchGate (AI Magazine 1986)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220605085_CYC_Using_Common_Sense_Knowledge_to_Overcome_Brittleness_and_Knowledge_Acquisition_Bottlenecks)
- **Moravec's paradox:** Hans Moravec, *Mind Children* (Harvard UP, 1988). Commonly cited wording: "it is comparatively easy to make computers exhibit adult level performance on intelligence tests or playing checkers, and difficult or impossible to give them the skills of a one-year-old when it comes to perception and mobility." Wording varies slightly across sources, so check the book before quoting. The rationale is that sensorimotor skills reflect millions of years of evolutionary optimization. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravec's_paradox); [Sketchplanations](https://sketchplanations.com/moravecs-paradox)
- **Critique of the paradox:** Arvind Narayanan argues the aphorism is "neither true nor useful" and that its evidence base is weak. — [Narayanan, AI Snake Oil / Normal Tech](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/fact-checking-moravecs-paradox)
- **A modern variant:** "Computer Use: Modern Moravec's Paradox" (Yu Su) argues that LLM agents excel at expert knowledge tasks yet struggle with mundane GUI or computer operation. — [Yu Su Substack](https://yusu.substack.com/p/computer-use-modern-moravecs-paradox)

### Inferences
- Cyc is the canonical case that *more declarative knowledge did not yield intelligence*. LLMs effectively reran the experiment with learned, not hand-coded, knowledge and got much further. ARC still suggests that knowledge alone does not give efficient novel adaptation.
- ARC-AGI-3's interactive games, which humans find easy and frontier LLMs initially found nearly impossible, are a contemporary Moravec-style inversion. Systems that win math gold failed simple exploratory game play that any untrained human solves.

### Gaps
- No verified source this session for Cyc's eventual scale (often cited as tens of millions of assertions and well over a thousand person-years) or for a retrospective verdict. Lenat & Marcus (2023), "Getting from Generative AI to Trustworthy AI: What LLMs might learn from Cyc" (arXiv:2308.04445), is the likely source but was not checked.
- No verified source on the expert-system "AI winter" economics (e.g., the late-1980s collapse of the Lisp-machine and expert-system market).

## 6. Quotes from leading researchers on knowledge vs. intelligence

### Takeaway
The verified quotes cluster into two camps. Chollet (and, more cautiously, Mitchell and LeCun) separate stored knowledge and memorized procedures from adaptive intelligence. Sutskever (and Hinton) argue that predicting and compressing human knowledge well requires something like understanding.

### Cited Findings
- **Chollet:**
  - "unlimited priors or unlimited training data allow experimenters to 'buy' arbitrary levels of skills for a system, in a way that masks the system's own generalization power." (2019) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/1911.01547v2)
  - "In the words of Jean Piaget, intelligence is what you use when you don't know what to do." And: "Memorization is useful, but intelligence is something else." — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/the-future/arc-prize-agi/)
  - "LLMs = 100% memorization... A LLM is a curve fitted to a dataset (that is to say, a memory)..." (Feb 2024) — [X](https://x.com/fchollet/status/1755270681359716611)
  - On o3: "a significant breakthrough in getting AI to adapt to novel tasks" but not AGI (Dec 2024). — [X](https://x.com/fchollet/status/1870169764762710376)
- **Sutskever:** "Predicting the next token well means that you understand the underlying reality that led to the creation of that token." (Dwarkesh Podcast, 2023) — [GreaterWrong transcript](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/FZkAG8Hezub7pWRM9/on-dwarkesh-s-3rd-podcast-with-tyler-cowen/comment/kboh4TyxGYFtRjjS3)
- **LeCun:** argues that a model trained only on text would never learn that pushing a table moves an object resting on it, because no text describes such tacit physical knowledge. — [Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Yann_LeCun). Secondary sources report his claim that LLMs fall short of cat-level intelligence and that GPT-style LLMs would be abandoned within about five years. — [Medium](https://ai-engineering-trend.medium.com/is-llm-leading-to-agi-feifei-li-yann-lecun-vs-hinton-ilya-sutskever-b0ec160fc41f) (paraphrase, secondary)
- **Hinton:** holds that LLMs genuinely grasp the meanings of words and ideas. After GPT-4 he described these models as approaching human intelligence, which LeCun publicly disputed. — [critical Substack summarizing Hinton's view](https://roborobert.substack.com/p/the-godfather-of-ai-doesnt-understand); [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3814630505422597) (secondary; no verbatim quote verified)
- **Melanie Mitchell:**
  - In "Abstraction and Analogy-Making in Artificial Intelligence" (Annals of the NY Academy of Sciences, 2021; arXiv:2102.10717) she argues that conceptual abstraction and analogy-making underlie humans' ability to learn, reason and robustly adapt knowledge to new domains, and that no current AI system is close to forming humanlike abstractions or analogies. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.10717) (paraphrase; verbatim not verified)
  - Her 2025 co-authored ConceptARC study concludes that accuracy alone "can substantially overestimate AI capabilities" because models use "surface-level 'shortcuts'". — [arXiv:2510.02125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02125)
  - She discussed counterfactual-task evidence on her Substack *AI: A Guide for Thinking Humans*. — [aiguide.substack.com](https://aiguide.substack.com/p/evaluating-large-language-models)
  - She gave a NeurIPS 2025 invited talk, "On the Science of 'Alien Intelligences': Evaluating Cognitive Capabilities in Babies, Animals, and AI". — [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/invited-talk/109607)
  - Her site lists a 2025 essay titled "Jagged Intelligence". — [melaniemitchell.me](https://melaniemitchell.me/)
- **Sutton:** "We want AI agents that can discover like we can, not which contain what we have discovered." (2019) — [essay](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html) [verbatim from canonical text; unverified this session]
- **Moravec (1988):** adult-level performance on "intelligence tests or playing checkers" is easy; "the skills of a one-year-old" in perception and mobility are hard. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravec's_paradox)

### Inferences
- For the thesis, Chollet's 2019 "buy... skill" sentence and his Piaget quote are the most quotable and best-sourced lines. Sutskever's next-token quote is the best-sourced counter-quote.
- Mitchell's "jagged intelligence" framing (superhuman on some knowledge-heavy tasks, brittle on simple novel ones) is a useful middle position for a synthesis.

### Gaps
- No verbatim Hinton quote verified (e.g., his 2023 statements that LLMs "really do understand"). Use only as paraphrase or check the original interview.
- LeCun's widely reported "dumber than a cat" comparison (e.g., WSJ, Oct 2024) was not verified from a primary source this session.
- Sutskever's late-2025 remarks that models "generalize dramatically worse than people" (Dwarkesh Podcast, Nov 2025) were not verified this session. They would be a strong pro-thesis quote from a scaling proponent if confirmed.
- No verbatim Mitchell quote from her Substack or "Jagged Intelligence" essay was verified.
